# Great Mosque of Tlemcen source handoff

The historical distinction matters: the mosque's Almoravid embellishment is dated 1136; the off-axis square minaret is a later Zayyanid addition around 1236. The model interprets the developed medieval building rather than attributing its minaret to 1136.

Museum With No Frontiers documents a rectangular courtyard with galleries, perpendicular prayer-hall naves and a higher poly-lobed central courtyard arch: https://islamicart.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=monument%3BISL%3Bdz%3BMon01%3B1%3Ben&pageD=N . Qantara explicitly separates Mosque 1136 from minaret 1236 and describes horseshoe, broken and poly-lobed arches and ornament concentrated at the mihrab: https://www.qantara-med.org/public/show_document.php?do_id=462&lang=en . Smarthistory describes thirteen aisles with six horseshoe arches perpendicular to the qibla: https://smarthistory.org/great-mosque-of-tlemcen/ . These are evidence for anatomy, not measured game-scale dimensions.

The original source compresses thirteen longitudinal aisles and six arches into a hollow prayer hall, with an open court, two arcaded galleries and an off-axis square minaret with restrained geometric plaster/stone panels. Flat terraced roofs and monumental alfiz provide the shared Maghrebi family style. Age IV adds sebka-inspired minaret relief, fitted cedar register and roof drains; this progression is a game interpretation, not a documented rebuilding phase. Unlike the ordinary Mosque source, the much denser 13-aisle prayer hall and tall minaret dominate; unlike the Wonder, the central body stays low and the large courtyard remains open.
