# Berber civic Town Center — source research and design

Primary sources inspected 2026-10-13:
- UNESCO, [Ksar of Ait-Ben-Haddou](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/444/): enclosed earthen settlement, regional pre-Saharan architecture. Used for rammed-earth massing and parapet silhouette; surviving structures are not asserted to be an Age I council building.
- UNESCO, [Medina of Marrakesh](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/331/): Almoravid foundation 1070–1072 and subsequent Almohad capital. Used as dynastic urban context, not a literal reconstruction.
- UNESCO, [Rabat](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1401/): surviving Almohad ramparts and monumental gates. Used for later civic gateway hierarchy.
- UNESCO, [Medina of Fez](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/170/): Marinid 13th–14th-century urban fabric includes palaces, residences and fondouks. Used for restrained Age IV geometric relief and council/trading courtyard context.

Original design: an enclosed council and trading courtyard with rammed-earth inhabited ranges, timber-fitted communal awning, unobstructed entrance and arcaded courtyard edge. Broad corner chambers are civic watch/storage rooms. No mosque dome or minaret is used. I: early earth/timber compound. II: formal stone gate footing. III: lime-coated dynastic courtyard with raised rear council chamber. IV: modest stone/earth geometric relief.
