# Funduq of Sijilmasa — 2026-10-13

The Northwestern University Block Museum's archaeological exhibition documents Sijilmasa as a vital Saharan gold-trade link and records Souk Ben Akla caravanserai west of the city: https://caravans.library.northwestern.edu/tour/saharan-frontiers/sijilmasa/ . This is evidence for the trade/inn function, not a measured reconstruction of this landmark.

Morocco's Ministry of Cultural Affairs and Museum With No Frontiers describe the Najjarin funduq's courtyard and upper floors: https://islamicart.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=monuments%3BISL%3Bma%3BMon01%3B17%3Ben . Najjarin is eighteenth century, later than the target: its functional type is comparative evidence only. The model's broad caravan gateway, two-storey room galleries, ground arcades, animal tethering, loading awnings, stair and storage stacks are an original medieval game-scale interpretation. The plain-earth → limework → carved joinery progression is art direction, not historical dating.

Unlike the small open Tribal Market, this landmark is a permanent grand enclosed caravan inn: four inhabited wings around a hollow working court, upper merchant lodging over storage/trading arcades, and a through gateway for loaded animals. No mosque/minaret silhouette or invented writing/emblem.
