# Ribat of the Sanhaja source design

Evidence: [UNESCO Medina of Sousse](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/498) identifies the ribat as both fort and religious building, a rectangular enclosure with a single southern entrance, courtyard and surrounding cells; its tower had a watch/signal role. [Tunisian National Heritage Institute](https://www.inp2020.tn/en/2020/04/07/le-ribat-de-sousse/) documents the near-square historical monument. These are building-type references, not evidence that the Sousse monument was a Sanhaja foundation.

Historical context: [OpenStax, People of the Sahel](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/15-3-the-people-of-the-sahel) describes the Sanhaja rulers of the Almoravid empire. [Metropolitan Museum, Almoravid and Almohad art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-almoravid-and-almohad-periods-ca-1062-1269) supports the Maghrebi material/art context. The specific game landmark is an original imagined Sanhaja frontier devotional and council community, not an archaeological reconstruction or a claim about a particular surviving ribat.

Anatomy/proportions: 5.8 square foundation inside actual 6x6 site_ext; two inhabited cell wings, three through-arches each; rear council/devotional arcade with three small openings; single southern entrance; attached southeast signal tower 1.12 wide, 3.15–3.85 high. Open courtyard, low flat roofs and fitted parapets deliberately distinguish the role from Keep. Age II retains simple earth and timber; III develops limestone entry piers/coping and lime roofs; IV extends joinery/drains and small geometric friezes. No national symbols or pseudo writing.
