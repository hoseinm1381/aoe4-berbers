## Research and art direction

UNESCO [Ait-Ben-Haddou](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/444/) documents traditional pre-Saharan Moroccan earth construction, wood, and geometric upper-wall decoration. Its extant buildings are mainly seventeenth century; this supplies a technique/material precedent, not a documented medieval stable plan. [FAO camel housing](https://www.fao.org/4/x6528e/x6528e09.htm) identifies roof shade, enclosure and water/feed troughs as functional housing elements; [FAO feeding/watering](https://www.fao.org/4/t0690e/t0690e09.htm) supports the watering/feed role. These are modern husbandry sources, not proof of a historic building plan. UNESCO [Tbourida](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tbourida-01483) describes Morocco's equestrian tradition from the sixteenth century, with dressed horses and riding equipment: context for restrained tack storage, not proof of medieval Berber cavalry equipment or specific stable form.

Three open-front connected stalls and rear mangers serve cavalry/camel production. Timber-supported shade, a hollow water basin and human tack/feed room face an open broad exit court. Age II is simple earth and timber/woven shelter; III adds lime, stone corners and structural joinery; IV adds modest geometric plaster relief and developed rest-apron rails. This is coherent Berber game art and age progression inferred from the function/material evidence, not an archaeological reconstruction.
